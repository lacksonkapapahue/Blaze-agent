# Shopper playbook

## Principles of the field
- The user's limits are filters, not suggestions: budget, country, delivery area, condition (new or used), brand and size are applied before ranking.
- Compare like with like: the same model, storage, size and condition, with the total price (item plus delivery plus fees) in one currency.
- Real listings only: every option is a page you opened, with a working link, a price as shown and stock status.
- Trust signals count: seller rating, return policy, warranty and whether the seller is official or a marketplace reseller.
- Never buy, add a payment method or check out; the job ends at a recommendation.

## Quality bar
A good shopping answer gives three to five real options that meet every limit, laid out on the same fields (price with currency, delivery fee and time to the user's location, stock, seller and rating, warranty or returns, link), and one clear pick with the reason in one sentence. Prices carry the date they were seen. Anything that broke a limit is left out or marked as such.

## Best practices
- Search the local shops first for the user's country (for example Game, Shoprite, Jumia in Zambia), then international ones that actually deliver there.
- Open each listing to read the exact variant, the price, delivery options and stock; do not trust category pages.
- Normalise: total cost to the door, same currency, same unit (per kg, per 128 GB).
- Check reviews for the specific model, not the brand; note any common fault.
- Watch for scams: prices far below the market, new sellers with no reviews, payment outside the platform.
- If nothing meets the budget, say so and give the closest option with how far over it is.

## Common mistakes to avoid
- Listing products that do not ship to the user's country or city.
- Comparing different variants (64 GB against 128 GB, refurbished against new) as if equal.
- Prices without currency, or item prices without delivery and fees.
- Dead or invented links, or links to search results instead of the product page.
- Recommending on price alone when the cheapest has bad ratings or no warranty.
- Going near checkout, sign-in or payment.

## Self-check
- [ ] Every option meets all the user's limits (budget, location, condition, variant).
- [ ] Each option has total price with currency, delivery, stock, seller rating and a product link I opened.
- [ ] Options are compared on the same variant and the same fields.
- [ ] There is one pick with a one-sentence reason.
- [ ] Prices carry the date seen; nothing was bought or added to a cart.

## Output format
"Pick: <product> at <shop> — <total price, currency> (<reason>)." Then a compact list or table of the options: Shop | Price | Delivery | Stock | Rating | Link. End with "Prices seen <date>." and any limit you could not meet.
