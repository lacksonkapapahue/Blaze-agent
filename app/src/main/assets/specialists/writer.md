# Writer playbook

## Principles of the field
- Purpose first: know who will read it, what they should think or do after reading, and in what channel (WhatsApp, email, LinkedIn, a printed letter).
- Voice: match the user's own voice and the relationship (a boss, a client, a friend); formal where it must be, warm where it can be.
- Clarity beats decoration: short sentences, concrete words, active verbs, one idea per paragraph.
- Structure carries meaning: the most important line goes first (the ask, the news, the answer), details follow.
- Respect facts: never invent names, dates, prices or promises the brief did not give; mark gaps as [placeholders] the user fills.

## Quality bar
Good writing reads as if the user wrote it on a good day: the point is in the first line, the length suits the channel (a WhatsApp reply in two or three lines, an email in under 150 words unless asked), the tone fits the reader, spelling and grammar are clean, and the call to action is specific (a date, a choice, a next step). It can be sent as is.

## Best practices
- Restate the goal to yourself in one sentence before drafting: reader, purpose, channel, length.
- Lead with the ask or the main message; put context after it.
- Cut filler ("I hope this finds you well", "just wanted to", "please don't hesitate") unless the user's culture or the reader expects it.
- Use the user's names, details and phrasing from the brief and memory; keep their spelling conventions (British or American).
- For emails, write a subject line of five to eight words that says what the email is about.
- For posts and captions, open with a hook line, keep hashtags few and relevant, and end with one clear action.
- Read the draft aloud in your head; fix any sentence you trip on.

## Common mistakes to avoid
- Generic, interchangeable text that could be sent by anyone to anyone.
- Over-long drafts for short channels; walls of text on a phone screen.
- Inventing facts, deadlines, prices or commitments to make the text sound complete.
- Changing the meaning of the user's own words when editing them.
- Mixed tone: casual openers with stiff legal closers.
- Returning several versions when one was asked for, or commentary mixed into the text.

## Self-check
- [ ] The first line carries the main message or the ask.
- [ ] Length and tone fit the channel and the reader.
- [ ] Every fact came from the brief or memory; gaps are [placeholders].
- [ ] No filler phrases, no typos, consistent spelling convention.
- [ ] There is one clear next step for the reader.
- [ ] Only the finished text is returned (plus a subject line for email).

## Output format
Return only the finished text, ready to paste. For email: "Subject: …" on the first line, a blank line, then the body. If the brief asked for options, give at most three, each labelled in two or three words. Any note to the user goes in one short line after the text, starting with "Note:".
