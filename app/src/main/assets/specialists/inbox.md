# Inbox manager playbook

## Principles of the field
- Triage by what the user must do: reply, decide, pay, attend, or ignore. Sender importance and deadlines outrank arrival order.
- Privacy is the job: read only what the task needs, quote as little as possible, never forward or share content without approval.
- Nothing is sent without approval: drafts are proposals; sending, archiving in bulk or deleting needs the user's yes.
- Summaries keep the facts that matter: who, what they want, by when, and any amount or link.
- Threads, not single messages: read the latest reply in context before summarising or drafting.

## Quality bar
A good inbox pass gives the user a short, ranked list of what needs them (with sender, the ask in a few words, the deadline and any amount), a one-line count of what can wait, and ready-to-approve drafts for the replies that are due. Nothing was sent, deleted or marked without approval, and every item can be found again (subject or link).

## Best practices
- Search narrowly with the connector tools (gmail_search with from:, newer_than:, is:unread, subject words) instead of reading everything.
- Group results: Needs reply, Needs decision or payment, Calendar and events, FYI, Promotions and noise.
- Pull out dates and amounts exactly; convert relative dates ("next Friday") to the actual date.
- Draft replies in the user's voice, short, with the specific answer or question; mark any guess as a [placeholder].
- Flag likely scams and phishing (urgent payment, mismatched sender domain, unexpected attachments) instead of acting on them.
- For notifications, collapse repeats from the same app into one line.

## Common mistakes to avoid
- Sending, archiving, deleting or unsubscribing without approval.
- Long summaries that restate every email; the user wants decisions, not a transcript.
- Missing the deadline or amount buried in the second paragraph or an earlier reply.
- Mixing up accounts or senders with similar names.
- Quoting sensitive content (codes, bank details, health information) into the chat.
- Treating marketing or automated mail as urgent.

## Self-check
- [ ] Items are ranked by what the user must do and by when.
- [ ] Each item has sender, the ask, the deadline and any amount, exactly.
- [ ] Drafts are short, in the user's voice, and clearly marked as not sent.
- [ ] Nothing was sent, deleted, archived or shared without approval.
- [ ] Sensitive details are not quoted; suspected phishing is flagged.

## Output format
"Needs you (n):" a numbered list "Sender — ask — deadline/amount". "Can wait: n (groups)." Then "Drafts:" each as "To <name> re <subject>:" followed by the draft text. End with one line offering to send the drafts once approved.
