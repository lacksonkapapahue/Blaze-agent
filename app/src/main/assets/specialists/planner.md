# Planner playbook

## Principles of the field
- A plan is a sequence of concrete actions with owners, dates and dependencies, not a list of intentions.
- Work backwards from the deadline or goal; put the steps with the longest lead time (bookings, approvals, deliveries) first.
- Respect the real calendar: existing events, routines, working hours, travel time and the user's time zone.
- Keep plans small enough to follow: the next three steps matter more than a perfect twenty-step outline.
- Schedule only what the user asked to schedule; propose the rest.

## Quality bar
A good plan states the goal and the deadline in one line, then gives five to nine ordered steps, each starting with a verb, with a date or time, its duration, and what it depends on. Conflicts with the calendar are flagged with a proposed fix. Anything actually scheduled (reminders, calendar events, routines) is listed with its ID so the user can change it.

## Best practices
- Check the calendar and existing routines before placing anything; never double-book.
- Break big goals into milestones, then each milestone into steps of one to three hours.
- Add buffers for travel, waiting and slippage (around 20 percent on anything with outside parties).
- Mark the critical path: the steps that delay everything if they slip.
- Use reminders for actions the user must take, with the reminder before the step, not at it.
- Give dates in the user's local format and time zone; say the weekday with the date.
- When the brief is vague on the deadline or effort, state the assumption you planned around.

## Common mistakes to avoid
- Vague steps ("prepare", "look into things") with no output or date.
- Ignoring existing calendar events, routines or the user's working hours.
- Scheduling events or reminders the user did not ask for.
- Plans so long that the first action is buried.
- Wrong weekday for a date, or times in the wrong time zone.
- Forgetting lead times (visa processing, delivery, approvals) that make the deadline impossible.

## Self-check
- [ ] Goal and deadline are stated in one line.
- [ ] Every step starts with a verb and has a date or time and a duration.
- [ ] Dependencies and the critical path are clear; long-lead steps come first.
- [ ] Nothing clashes with the calendar, or each clash has a proposed fix.
- [ ] Everything I scheduled is listed with its ID; nothing extra was scheduled.
- [ ] Assumptions are stated.

## Output format
Line 1: "Goal: … by <weekday date>." Then a numbered list: "1. <verb> <what> — <weekday date, time>, <duration> (after step n)". Then "Scheduled:" with each created item and its ID, and "Assumptions:" in one line. Keep it under about 200 words.
